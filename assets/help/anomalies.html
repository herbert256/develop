Anomalies

What this shows

Hours and days that behaved unlike their usual selves, in two tables on one page: Daily first — the coarse read of which days were odd — and Hourly below it, paged, because the episodes run into the hundreds. The Hourly table compares every (day, hour) bucket of the transfer log against that hour-of-day's typical value — the median across the window's days, with weekdays and weekends baselined separately, so a busy Tuesday 10:00 is judged against other 10:00s on weekdays and a quiet Sunday is never flagged "silent" against a Tuesday. Consecutive flagged hours merge into one episode ("Duration high 08:00–14:59"), bridging a single unflagged hour so a run with one calm hour in the middle stays one row. The Daily table applies the same idea to whole calendar days against the typical day (same weekday/weekend baselines) — it catches the day-sized events an hourly view fragments, like a day that moved 4× the usual files or ran a whole day at an elevated error rate.

The signals

Hourly — all bucket by the hour a File started. An hour flags when it is at least 4× its typical and clears an absolute floor (so near-zero baselines cannot turn noise into alarms): Error rate — ≥25% of the hour's Files failed, at least 5 Files; Duration — the average duration of the hour's OK Files is ≥5 minutes, at least 5 OK Files (failures are excluded, as on the Performance report); Files spike — ≥30 Files; Silence — zero Files in an hour that typically moves ≥20 (a stopped feed); Volume — ≥100 MB moved.

Daily — day totals swing less than hours, so the count-like signals flag earlier: Error rate (≥10%, ≥20 Files, ≥4× typical), Duration (average per OK File ≥5 min, ≥20 OK Files, ≥4×), Files spike (≥100 Files, ≥2×), Files drop (≤¼ of a typical that is ≥100 Files), Silence (a calendar day with no transfers at all where ≥20 are typical — missing days are found by walking the calendar, since a fully-dead day leaves no log rows), and Volume (≥200 MB, ≥2×).

Reading the tables

Value (Daily) and Peak (Hourly, the worst flagged hour in the episode) are the flagged figure, × typical that figure against the baseline. Red rows are ≥10× typical, or a silence; orange the rest. Files/Error count the Files started (and failed) inside the flagged window. The Date link opens that day's page with the matching hero-chart view pre-selected, so the episode is immediately visible in context.

Coming from a day page

A day page carries an Anomaly scan entry in its transfer problem list only when that day was flagged, and it says how many findings the day has. A clean day simply has no such entry — the absence is the answer, where the always-present link it replaced could only be followed to find nothing. Following it opens this page narrowed to that one day, so both tables show that day's findings alone; Reset widens back to the full window.

Caveats

On the newest day the outbound legs of just-arrived files may not be in the export yet, which can flag the late hours as an error-rate episode — recheck after the next export. A duration episode often pairs with a later Files/Volume spike: files queueing for hours and then flushing at once show up as "slow morning, busy afternoon" — the two rows describe one event. Thresholds are deliberately conservative; an empty report means no hour beat 4× its own routine, not that nothing happened.
