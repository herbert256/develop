Ranking

What this shows

Where every entity stands against the others of its own type, on the five metrics its detail page ranks it by. Each metric is two columns — the position and the value behind it — and a divider separates the five pairs.

The five rankings

  • Files — most Files first, so #1 is the busiest entity of its type.
  • Volume — most bytes first. A flow can be #1 by volume and unremarkable by file count (one big file a day), which is exactly the kind of mismatch this page is for.
  • Errors — highest error percentage first, so here a low number is bad news: #1 is the worst-performing entity of its type, and a flawless one sorts last.
  • Duration — shortest average transfer first (#1 = fastest).
  • Throughput — highest bytes per second first (#1 = fastest).

Who is ranked

The entities the detail pages rank: those seen with real transfers. A server-log-only (blue) entity has no metric to stand on and is left out, as it is on its own page. An entity whose transfers were never timed shows - for Duration and Throughput but still ranks on the other three.

Picking a type

The second button row switches between the seven entity types — subscriptions, accounts, logins, hosts, partners, applications, domains. Ranks never cross types: a subscription is ranked among subscriptions only.

How it is computed

The positions are not calculated here. Each entity’s detail page writes its own five positions to a sidecar as it is rendered, and this page collates them — so the report and the detail pages are the same numbers by construction.

Arriving from a detail page

Every row of an entity’s own Ranking box links here. The link opens the page for that entity’s type, sorted on the metric whose row was clicked (#1 at the top), turned to the page holding that entity and scrolled to its row, which is drawn with an outline. The point is the neighbourhood: a bare #138 says little, the twenty rows around it say whether the field is tightly packed or the entity is an outlier.

Such a link always opens at the full period, whatever range you last set elsewhere — the position you clicked is a full-period figure, and answering with a different number would make the link a lie. Your remembered range is left alone; picking a new one here saves as usual.

The date filter re-ranks the page

At the full range the page shows those baked figures. Narrow From/To and both halves of every pair are recomputed over the selected days alone: the values re-aggregate and the positions renumber with them, so you never read a filtered value beside a full-period rank. An entity with nothing in the range drops out of the table and out of the population the positions are counted over — which is the point of the filter, since it shows who mattered last week rather than who has accumulated the most since logging began. Widening back to the full range restores the baked numbers exactly.

Two rules survive the recomputation unchanged: a tie shares a position (the next ones are skipped), and a flawless entity still sorts last on Errors instead of first. Duration and Throughput are averages over the timed transfers in the range, so an entity that has files but nothing timed there shows - for both and is left out of those two rankings.
