Cluster & Service Health

What this shows

The platform's own infrastructure story — the events that never reach the transfer logs because no transfer is involved. Four tables:

  • Daemon & service events — the protocol daemons starting and stopping ("Starting SSH server with name Ssh Default.", "Stopping PeSIT server …", "Daemon SSH stopped.", the bind/listener lines) plus the watchdog's "Service … appears to be unresponsive or stopped" warnings. A daemon restart logs a small burst of these lines; the counts tell you how often the daemons cycled.
  • Cluster distress signals — low-volume but high-signal warnings from the streaming/cluster layer: Oracle Coherence cluster-formation delays, "No peer has been selected by the dispatch policy", "Streaming not ready yet", and streaming completion-token timeouts.

How it's computed

Each message family is matched and counted per signal, with first/last dates and the click-to-expand drill of the 10 most recent raw lines. Counts are additive and re-total under the date filter.

Why it matters

A daemon that restarted a dozen times in a month, or a watchdog that keeps finding a service unresponsive, are early warnings of infrastructure trouble — and they are invisible everywhere else on this site by design: the transfer logs only see transfers. The steady background rate is the baseline; what deserves attention is a change — restarts clustering on one day, or a distress signal appearing that was never there.

About this report

The health of the platform itself on one page — the infrastructure story the transfer figures never show: daemon lifecycle and cluster distress signals, stuck internal events (expired heartbeats and the recovery sweeps) and scheduler overruns (tasks skipped because the previous run was still going).
