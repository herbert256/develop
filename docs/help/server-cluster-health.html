Cluster & Service Health

What it shows

The platform’s own infrastructure story — events the transfer logs never see: daemon lifecycle and cluster distress, stuck internal events and scheduler overruns. One row per message family (signal), with its count and first/last dates.

Sections

  • Daemon & service events — the protocol daemons starting and stopping (“Starting SSH server with name Ssh Default.”, “Stopping PeSIT server …”, “Daemon SSH stopped.”, the bind/listener lines) plus the watchdog’s “Service … appears to be unresponsive or stopped” warnings. One restart logs a small burst (stop/shutdown request, then start, bind, listener); the counts say how often the daemons cycled.
  • Cluster distress signals — low-volume, high-signal warnings from the streaming/cluster layer: Oracle Coherence cluster-formation delays, “No peer has been selected by the dispatch policy”, “Streaming not ready yet”, streaming completion-token timeouts. A sustained rise deserves attention before it becomes transfer failures.
  • Stuck internal events — expired heartbeats and the recovery sweeps (empty where an environment has none).
  • Scheduler overruns — the TM warnings “The task … is still in progress. Skipping the next scheduled occurrence of this task.” per subscription (the last segment of the logged subscription folder), shown as logged; a name matching a known account or subscription links to its detail page.

Rules

  • Counts are additive and re-total under the date filter. Click a signal or row for its 10 most recent raw lines.
  • The steady background rate is the baseline; what matters is change — restarts clustering on one day, a distress signal that was never there, a daemon restarting a dozen times a month, a watchdog repeatedly finding a service unresponsive.
