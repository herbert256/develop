Failure Heatmap

What this shows

Where the Load by Hour and Load by Weekday reports show volume, this one focuses on failures and combines both axes. Three views over logical transfers (one per CoreId, delivered outcome):

  • By hour of day — Files, Error and Error % for each of the 24 hours, with a bar scaled to the busiest failure hour.
  • By weekday — the same, by day of week (Monday first).
  • Hour × weekday failure heatmap — a 24×7 grid where each cell is the number of failed transfers for that hour on that weekday, tinted by quartile (darker = more failures). This is where a recurring failure window jumps out — a Monday-10:00 batch, a nightly poll — instead of being averaged away.

How it's computed

Every logical transfer is placed by the hour of its start time and the weekday of its date (from the Julian day number, 0 = Monday); a transfer counts as failed when its delivered (final-row) outcome is not OK. The hour and weekday tables re-aggregate under the date filter and their Error counts drill to the 10 most recent failed Files for that bucket; the heatmap re-sums and re-tints every cell for the selected range.

Why it matters

A daily failure total can completely hide a sharp temporal pattern. If nearly all of a day's failures land in one hour, or every Monday morning when a batch kicks off, that points straight at the schedule, job or upstream feed responsible — a very different investigation from a steady background failure rate. Because one dominant window can make every other cell look pale, narrowing the date range (which re-tints the grid) is the quickest way to see the shape of a normal day versus an incident.
