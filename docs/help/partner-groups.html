Partner groups & the Direction column

What a partner group is

Most partner names on the Entities → Partners pages are a single organisation — ROTAFORM, VECOZO. Some are a group: a name like GBA_TT_XXLLNC that joins several partner codes with underscores. FlowManager configures those codes on separate logical flows, but the grouping logic recognises them as one real partner organisation and counts them together. A group row carries a small 🔗 link icon after its name; clicking it opens a page that lists the group's member codes and the exact evidence used to merge them.

How partners are grouped

Every three-part logical flow name reads domain_application_partner, so its LAST part is a partner token. Those tokens are then merged into one group whenever any of four things holds:

  • Their logical flows connect to the same configured host — two flows delivering to (or collecting from) one endpoint belong to one partner.
  • Their logical flows whitelist the same IP — two inbound flows allowing the same source address are the same partner reaching us two ways.
  • One partner's host resolves to an IP another whitelists — the endpoint SecureTransport dials out to lands on an address an inbound flow's whitelist allows: the same partner, both directions.
  • A curated alias pair (input/<env>/partner-aliases.tsv) declares two tokens one organisation — the hand-maintained knowledge the infrastructure cannot prove.

The group's name is its member codes joined with underscores, sorted alphabetically — unless the alias file supplies a canonical name every member aliases (the star), which then names the group. The per-group page groups the raw evidence by the pair of partner codes it links, showing one representative fact per pair with a count of the rest.

The Direction column

The Partners list carries a Direction column reading connection / movement — for example both/both or in/out. Connection is the partner's configured side: in (the partner connects into SecureTransport), out (SecureTransport connects out to the partner) or both. Movement is which way the files actually travel across the partner's subscriptions (in, out, or both). The two diverge on pull flows — a partner SecureTransport connects out to in order to collect files that move in reads out/in.
