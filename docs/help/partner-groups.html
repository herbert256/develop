Partner groups & the Direction column

What it shows

Most partner names on Entities → Partners are one organisation (ROTAFORM, VECOZO). A group such as GBA_TT_XXLLNC joins several partner codes with underscores: FlowManager configures those codes on separate logical flows, the grouping recognises them as one real organisation and counts them together.

Rules

  • Every three-part logical flow name reads domain_application_partner, so its last part is a partner token.
  • Tokens merge into one group when any of three things holds: their logical flows connect to the same configured host; their logical flows whitelist the same IP (the same partner reaching us two ways); one partner’s host resolves to an IP another whitelists (the same partner, both directions).
  • Every rule is derived from the configuration. Hand-maintained knowledge the infrastructure cannot prove (“this variant code is really that organisation”) is a part replacement in input/logical_partners.txt: the variant is rewritten to its canonical token before it ever becomes a partner, so the two never form a group.
  • The group’s name is its member codes joined with underscores, sorted alphabetically.

Tips

  • A group row carries a 🔗 icon after its name; it opens a page listing the member codes and the evidence used to merge them, grouped by the pair of codes it links — one representative fact per pair with a count of the rest.

The Direction column

  • The Partners list reads connection / movement, e.g. both/both or in/out.
  • Connection — the partner’s configured side: in (the partner connects into SecureTransport), out (SecureTransport connects out to the partner) or both.
  • Movement — which way the files actually travel across the partner’s subscriptions: in, out or both.
  • The two diverge on pull flows: SecureTransport connects out to collect files that move in — out/in.
