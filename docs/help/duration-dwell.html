Duration distribution & Store-and-forward

What it shows

Two clocks on the same Files, side by side: the Duration distribution (left) is the whole trip of a File; the Dwell-time distribution (right) is one piece of it — the wait inside SecureTransport between the inbound leg completing and the outbound leg starting. Below: the dwell per subscription and the gap per day.

Duration distribution

  • A histogram of the Files by wall-clock duration (first record start to last record end, gaps and retry idle included) in seven bands: up to 100 ms, 1 s, 10 s, 1 min, 5 min, 30 min and beyond.
  • OK transfers (default) counts delivered Files only; All transfers adds the failed ones, whose duration is how long they ran before giving up — mostly instant 0-byte attempts, piling up in the first band.
  • Files = the count in the band, Share = its part of the scope’s Files; a From/To range re-aggregates both from the per-day buckets each row carries.

Store-and-forward

  • SecureTransport is a store-and-forward hub: a file arrives on an inbound leg, is held, then leaves on an outbound leg. No other report measures that wait (Transfer Duration measures the end-to-end span).
  • Dwell-time distribution — bands under 1 s, 1–5 s, 5–30 s, 30–60 s, 1–5 min, over 5 min; most files forward almost immediately, the tail is where latency lives. Re-aggregates over the selected dates.
  • Dwell by subscription — average and maximum dwell per subscription, slowest first; Avg and Max re-aggregate. A click opens its 10 most recent Files.
  • Gap per day — per calendar day the Min / Median (= P50) / Max and P25–P99 of the in→out gap, for delivered Files with exactly one Inbound and one Outbound record, both Processed (retries and failed legs excluded); banked under the inbound date, in whole ms / s / m / h.
  • The per-day figures are not additive: a narrowed range keeps each day but blanks the totals.
  • The intro states the measurable Files, their median, p95 and max dwell, and how many overlapping Files were excluded.

Rules

  • Dwell = the earliest outbound start minus the latest inbound completion (inbound start + duration), over the records sharing one CoreId.
  • Measurable only with both an inbound completion and an outbound start; an outbound leg starting before the inbound finished (overlapping/streamed legs) gives a negative gap and is excluded, counted in the intro.
  • Timestamps become seconds through a Julian-day-number helper; percentiles use the nearest-rank method.
  • A CoreId takes the subscription of its first record carrying one; a CoreId without any subscription is dropped from the cache.
  • A growing dwell — invisible in both leg durations — is an early warning of queue pressure, a slow or backed-up outbound target or a scheduling problem, before a failure or SLA breach; the per-subscription table names the flow, p95 and max size the tail.

Tips

  • Per-day statistics and percentiles: Transfer Duration; the individual slowest transfers: Longest Files.
