Accounts in boxes

What this shows

Every account configured in FlowManager, boxed by what is true of the subscriptions connected to it. It is the account-level reading of Subscriptions in boxes: the boxes are the same twenty, and an account is in a box when at least one of its subscriptions is. One more box, No subscriptions, exists only here.

The question it answers is the one a subscription list cannot: which accounts should I be worried about? An account is usually the unit you act on — it is what a partner is given, what a credential belongs to, what someone owns — while a subscription is one flow through it. An account with four flows shows up four times on the subscription page and once here, with every signal across those flows gathered into one row.

How to read a row

An account can be in several boxes at once, and that is the interesting case. A row that is both Error and Went quiet is an account where one flow fails and another stopped carrying traffic — the sort of thing that looks fine from the account’s own traffic figures and is not. Boxes are not exclusive and are not a verdict, with one exception: OK holds only the accounts whose site-wide result is green — every connected subscription green — so OK is the account-level verdict, and an account with one delivering flow beside a failing one lands in Error instead. The row tint (green / orange / red / blue) is that same site-wide result.

The page opens on the OK box. Click a box to narrow the table to it — every column with nothing left to show is hidden, and the note under the boxes always explains the box you picked. In the problem rows the orange boxes (attention, or not working yet) come before the red ones (failing).

Each flagged cell opens the subscription report behind that flag, already searched for the specific subscriptions that put this account in the box. So clicking only red on an account with six flows lands you on Only red showing just the ones of those six that are red — not the account, and not all six. There is deliberately no account version of those reports.

How it’s computed

The page computes nothing of its own. It takes the twenty box memberships exactly as Subscriptions in boxes computes them — the same shared code path, so the two can never drift — and joins them onto accounts.

The join is the FlowManager configuration, never the name. Accounts and subscriptions are connected through the exported configuration cache (xref/_subscriptions-accounts.tsv), which is the actual wiring. The two look related by name, because a subscription is commonly called UC1_<account>_<account>, and matching on that would appear to work — but a naming convention is not a guarantee, and the separator twins (FRE-SAPCD-X and FRE_SAPCD_X are two different accounts) are exactly where a loose match silently merges two entities into one row.

No subscriptions is the account with no row in that cache at all: nothing references it, so nothing can ever route a file through it. It is configured and inert. The other twenty boxes have nothing to say about such an account, which is why it needs a box of its own — without it those accounts would sit in no box and the Total would stop meaning “every account is in at least one box”. The same condition puts the amber banner on the account’s detail page and lists it under “Account without subscriptions” on Config hygiene.

Where the boxes come from

Eight are read from a report’s own data file (From green to red, Trouble after success, Only red, No Dir, No Files, Missing cron, Went quiet, Connection failures); Deploy comes from the Deploy errors report; three are derived from the transfer cache (One-legged, Waiting, Expired); and three have no report at all (OK, Not seen, Server log only) and are read from the same sources the Entities views are built from. See the Subscriptions in boxes help for what each one means in detail — the meanings are identical, only the subject changes.

Connection failures and Deploy are unresolved-only: a subscription whose last such event was followed by an OK File has recovered and is not boxed. That filter is what makes those two mean “still broken” rather than “has ever broken”.

Reading the combinations

Only red plus Connection failures or Deploy is the strongest signal on the page: a flow that has never worked, with the server log saying why — we cannot connect, or the route was abandoned. OK plus Went quiet is the quiet killer: the last file was delivered, so nothing looks wrong, and nothing has come since. No subscriptions on its own is not an incident at all, it is cleanup.
