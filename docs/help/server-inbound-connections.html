Inbound Connections

What this shows

Who connects in to SecureTransport, over which protocol, from where — from the "User with login name "…", associated with account "…", had initiated a connection over SSH|PESIT|FTP. Remote address: …" lines the server logs for every inbound connection. Five tables:

  • Connections by protocol — the volume per protocol. This is the only place FTP and PeSIT inbound connection volume exists on this site.
  • Connections per day — the daily SSH / PESIT / FTP trend. A protocol falling silent, or a sudden spike, shows here first.
  • By account — connections per account, with its protocol mix and the distinct source addresses seen. An account known from the transfer logs links to its detail page.
  • By source address — the partners' SOURCE addresses (what actually connects in — the address a whitelist allows), top 50 by connections.
  • Whitelist policy usage — which Login Restriction Policy the screening actually matched, from the "Allowed user … corresponding policy name '…'" lines.

How it's computed

One count per connection line — this is connection volume, before any authentication or transfer happens. The distinct-count columns (Addresses per account, Accounts per address/policy) cover the whole period and do not re-aggregate under the date filter; the connection counts do. Click a row for its 10 most recent connection lines.

How it relates to the other connection reports

Three reports look at inbound sessions from different angles: Logon is the screening funnel (allowed / disallowed / no-account / lockouts per login), Auth Activity counts successful SSH authentications, and this report counts raw connections — every protocol, before and regardless of authentication. Comparing them is informative: many connections with few authentications means clients connecting and failing (or probing); connections from an address the whitelist table doesn't know is worth a look.

Host-key mismatches lists every presented-vs-expected SSH fingerprint pair from the “Wrong server fingerprint” warnings — one presented key against many stored keys means a server-side key rotation (or something worth a closer look). Test outcomes breaks the explicit test connections down by their error reason.

About this report

Both directions of the connection story in one report. Inbound: who connects in to SecureTransport, over which protocol, from which addresses, and how the whitelist policies are used. Outbound: why and where our own connections fail — the failure-reason breakdown, the per-remote-host view and the test connections.

One count per "had initiated a connection over <protocol>" line. FTP and PeSIT inbound volume appears in no other report. Counts are additive and re-total under the date filter.

The daily protocol mix. A protocol falling silent, or a sudden connection spike from one day to the next, stands out here first.

Connections per account (as logged; an account known from the transfer logs links to its detail page). Addresses is the distinct source addresses seen for that account over the whole period — it does not re-aggregate under the date filter. Click an account for its 10 most recent connection lines.

The partners' SOURCE addresses (what actually connects in — the address the whitelist allows), not the configured outbound endpoints. Accounts is the distinct accounts seen from that address over the whole period. Top 50 by connections; the total row sums the shown rows. Click an address for its 10 most recent connection lines.

Which Login Restriction Policy the "Allowed user … corresponding policy name" screening lines actually matched — the policies doing the work, next to Logon's per-login funnel. Accounts is the distinct accounts allowed under that policy (whole period).

The reason is classified from the failure tail. "SSH exception (generic)" is a com.maverick.ssh.SshException without a more specific cause (often a reset/aborted SSH connection); Timeout, Bad certificate, Incompatible security protocols and Algorithm negotiation each point at a different fix. Failures and share re-aggregate over the selected dates.

The physical partner endpoint (host, not subscription — a host can serve several subscriptions), with the reason that dominates its failures. Shown as logged; a host with a detail page links to it (matched case-insensitively — the canonical endpoint spelling is lowercase). Click a host for its 10 most recent failures.

Explicit "Performs test connection for <protocol> protocol" checks — an admin/API testing a partner configuration before real transfers.

The "Wrong server fingerprint: got X, expected Y" warnings, per fingerprint pair: a partner endpoint presenting an SSH host key that does not match the stored known-host entry. One presented key (`07`) accounts for 26 of the 26 warning(s), checked against 1 different expected keys — ONE endpoint presenting a NEW key that many stored entries no longer match (a server-side key rotation), not many endpoints drifting at once. A short "got" value is the log itself truncating; shown as logged. Click a pair for its 10 most recent warnings.

How the explicit test connections failed: the "Error during test connection" reason tail, as logged.
