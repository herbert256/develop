Route throughput

What it shows

Wire speed in MB/s per route (subscription × protocol × direction), slowest routes first, with a per-protocol rollup and a trend.

Rules

  • Only legs that measure a real rate count: duration over 500 ms and size over 1 MB; the floors drop legs whose rate is mostly session setup and rounding.
  • MB/s = the route’s counted bytes divided by its summed leg durations — volume-weighted, so one big slow file outweighs ten quick ones.
  • A leg’s duration is the record’s own wire time; the store-and-forward dwell between legs is never included (the Store-and-forward report measures that).
  • Trend compares first-half with second-half MB/s of the window; shown when both halves have at least 3 Transfers.
  • Compare a route to its protocol rollup, not across protocols: PeSIT throttles far below SFTP by design — a PeSIT route at 0.2 MB/s and an SFTP route at 40 MB/s are both normal; a route far below its protocol peers is worth a look.
  • routing is the internal store-and-forward leg between the inbound and outbound sides.
